\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Cross-domain learning often reuses network motifs without reusing the
\emph{falsification checks} that make an inductive bias scientific.
We formulate a minimal \emph{Huygens learning protocol}: (i)~entities as
secondary sources, (ii)~a distance kernel, (iii)~gather and readout, and
(iv)~gates that scramble or occlude the intended distance.
The same slots transfer across three public benchmarks with
\emph{domain-appropriate} distance.
On STEAD seismic phase picking, the Huygens axis is inter-sample time; time
shuffle collapses mean P/S F1 from $0.980$ to $<0.01$.
On RadHAR mmWave activity recognition, the axis is inter-frame time while
points within a frame remain a simultaneous snapshot; a Wave+Pattern model
matches a BiGRU baseline ($0.979$ vs $0.979$) and frame shuffle drops accuracy
to $0.532$.
On QM9 HOMO--LUMO \gap{} regression, the protocol becomes a geometric kernel on
atomic distances and approaches a matched continuous-filter baseline
(MAE $0.0063$ vs $0.0052$ Hartree), while coordinate scramble, first-neighbour
shell occlusion, and feature-space distance controls all degrade performance.
We claim a transferable \emph{protocol language}, not a universal spacetime
wave backbone; maps of when inter-frame Huygens is necessary versus redundant
are deferred to a companion study.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
Modern scientific ML frequently exports architectures across sensors---CNNs,
Transformers, message-passing GNNs---while leaving the \emph{meaning} of
distance implicit.
When a method is described as ``wave-like'' or ``physics-inspired'', reviewers
rightly ask: what quantity is being treated as distance, and what happens when
that quantity is destroyed?

We answer with a protocol rather than a single backbone.
A \textbf{Huygens learning protocol} fills five slots (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}):
secondary sources; pairwise distance $d_{ij}$; a Huygens-style kernel
$K(d_{ij})$; gather/readout; and pre-registered gates that break $d_{ij}$.
The contribution is that these slots can be instantiated consistently on
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item seismic waveforms (STEAD)\cite{mousavi2019stead},
\item mmWave frame sequences (RadHAR)\cite{singh2019radhar}, and
\item static molecular graphs (QM9)\cite{ramakrishnan2014qm9},
\end{enumerate}
without forcing one physical delay story onto every modality.

\paragraph{Simultaneous frames.}
A modelling commitment that avoids a common category error:
\emph{within one acquisition frame (or one time index), spatial sites are
simultaneous}.
Huygens acts along \emph{inter-frame / inter-sample} time when a sequence
exists.
Static molecules have no frame axis; they use Euclidean $r_{ij}$ as a
\emph{geometric variant} of the same protocol language.
We therefore do \emph{not} claim resolved electromagnetic delays among points
inside a single RadHAR cloud, nor light-cone causality on QM9.

\paragraph{Related work (brief).}
Seismic picking networks (PhaseNet, EQTransformer)\cite{zhu2019phasenet,mousavi2020eqtransformer}
and radar HAR models provide strong temporal baselines.
SchNet-style continuous filters\cite{schutt2018schnet} are the natural geometric
baseline for molecules.
Our emphasis differs: not beating every baseline by a point, but showing that a
shared Huygens \emph{slot language} plus gates travels across domains.

\section*{Huygens protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\paragraph{Slots.}
Let entities $\{i\}$ carry source embeddings $s_i$.
A kernel depends on a declared distance $d_{ij}$:
\begin{equation}
K_{ij}=K(d_{ij};\,\theta),\qquad
h_i \leftarrow h_i + \mathrm{Agg}_j\!\big(K_{ij}\,h_j\big),
\end{equation}
with optional medium weights $\rho_i$ and a task head on pooled fields.
Gates intervene on $d_{ij}$ at test time (or retrain with wrong distance) while
holding architecture and compute roughly fixed.

\paragraph{Temporal instantiation.}
$d_{ij}$ is lag along the sequence axis $t$.
Spatial coordinates at fixed $t$ may enter a readout (e.g.\ energy patterns) but
are not treated as intra-frame travel times.

\paragraph{Geometric instantiation.}
$d_{ij}=\|R_i-R_j\|_2$ with cutoff $r_c$ (molecules).
Amplitude may use an RBF filter bank (matched to continuous-filter baselines)
while phase/envelope retain a Huygens form.

\paragraph{Pre-registered gates.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{STEAD:} time shuffle / block shuffle / circular shift; pass if
mean P/S F1 drops by $\ge 0.10$ absolute or $\ge 25\%$ relative.
\item \textbf{RadHAR:} frame shuffle ($\ge 5$\,pp drop); history truncation
(clean $\ge T{=}60 \ge T{=}30$ and clean${-}T30\ge 2$\,pp).
\item \textbf{QM9:} H1 competitive with matched CFConv (M1); scramble MAE $>$
clean; shell-1 occlusion hurts at least as much as shell-2; feature-distance
control worse than geometric H1.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Protocol gates across three domains}
Figure~\ref{fig:gates} and Table~\ref{tab:gates} summarise the locked numbers
(\texttt{outputs/nmi\_paper\_b/NUMBERS.json}).

\paragraph{STEAD.}
On a multi-scale Huygens--Fresnel picking checkpoint, clean mean P/S F1 is
$0.980$.
Permuting the time axis of $x\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times 3}$ (labels fixed) collapses
the score to $6\times 10^{-4}$; block shuffle and large circular shifts likewise
fail (gate \textbf{pass}).
This is the temporal analogue of a geometry scramble: the model cannot retain
picking skill without ordered time.

\paragraph{RadHAR.}
Wave+Pattern (T$=$120 frames) reaches $0.979$ accuracy on the official test
split, matching BiGRU ($0.979$) and WaveGRU ($0.980$).
Frame shuffle drops accuracy to $0.532$; shortening history to T$=$30 yields
$0.821$ (gates \textbf{pass}).
Time reversal alone is mild ($0.961$), consistent with activity cues that are
partly reversible; the protocol knife is unordered frames and insufficient
history, not every temporal transform.

\paragraph{QM9 \gap{} (full, $n{=}133{,}885$).}
Geometric Huygens H1 reaches MAE $0.0063$ Hartree versus CFConv M1 $0.0052$ and
no-geometry M0 $0.0295$ (H1 within ${\sim}22\%$ of M1, ${\sim}4.7\times$ better
than M0).
Scramble MAE $0.090$; shell-1 occlusion $0.092$ versus shell-2 $0.013$;
feature-distance training $0.039$ (gates \textbf{pass}).

\subsection*{RadHAR: matched baseline, not a leaderboard chase}
Accuracy among WaveGRU, Wave+Pattern, and BiGRU is saturated near $98\%$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:radhar}).
The scientific payload is therefore (i)~parity with a strong temporal baseline
and (ii)~frame-shuffle collapse, plus an optional spatial-pattern readout that
separates jack/jump via named Doppler-width statistics.
Near$\to$far ``propagation along range'' alone underperforms; distributional
spatial energy is the useful geometric complement to inter-frame Huygens.

\subsection*{QM9: geometric variant and a negative control}
Figure~\ref{fig:qm9} shows primary and gate bars for \gap{}.
Internal energy U0 on the same full split is reported in Extended Data
(Fig.~\ref{fig:u0}): H1 MAE $2.69$ versus M1 $0.70$.
Thus the protocol can fail a target even when geometry gates still move---a
useful reminder that ``Huygens slots filled'' $\neq$ ``any label solved''.

\section*{Discussion}
What transfers is a \emph{discipline}: declare $d_{ij}$, implement $K(d_{ij})$,
and publish gates that break $d_{ij}$.
What does not transfer is a single delay ontology.
Sequences use inter-frame time; co-temporal point sets do not invent travel
times inside the frame; molecules substitute geometry for time.

This framing also clarifies negative results.
A prior mmWave regime study that asked whether \emph{intra-bin electromagnetic
delay} selects a wave prior is a different question from the present
inter-frame protocol; the two should not be conflated.
Likewise, EEG snapshots at a fixed time index are simultaneous across channels
under the same rule.

\paragraph{Companion Paper~A.}
When is inter-frame Huygens \emph{necessary} rather than merely usable?
That requires a regime map (process timescale versus frame interval, causal
versus anti-causal kernels) and is intentionally out of scope here.

\section*{Methods}
\paragraph{STEAD.}
We evaluate a multi-scale Huygens--Fresnel picking model
(\texttt{outputs/run28/28\_ms\_fresnel\_phys\_50ep\_local/best.pt}) on the
standard test split ($n{=}126{,}566$ windows in the logged null run).
Waveforms are $x\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times 3}$ with $T{=}400$.
Nulls permute or reverse the \emph{time} axis (dimension~1) while keeping
detection/pick labels fixed.
Metrics: detection F1 and P/S picking F1; primary score is mean P/S F1
(``pick focus'').

\paragraph{RadHAR.}
Public RadHAR official Train/Test split\cite{singh2019radhar}; rich
$312$-channel windows; claim model Wave~v3 + spatial pattern late fusion at
T$=$120 (${\sim}4$\,s at $30$\,Hz).
Baselines: WaveGRU and BiGRU at the same $T$.
Nulls: independent frame permutation per window; front-zero history truncation
to the last $60$ or $30$ frames.

\paragraph{QM9.}
Figshare XYZ molecules\cite{ramakrishnan2014qm9}; full cache $n{=}133{,}885$ with
fixed seed split ($107{,}108$/$13{,}388$/$13{,}389$).
Primary target: HOMO--LUMO \gap{} (Hartree).
M0: bag-of-atoms MLP; M1: continuous-filter convolution (SchNet-style RBF
filter); H1: Huygens phase/envelope $\times$ RBF amplitude with cutoff
$5$\,\AA{} (parameter count ${\sim}55$k vs M1 ${\sim}34$k).
Gates: Gaussian coordinate scramble; occlude neighbour shells
$[0,1.8]$\,\AA{} vs $(1.8,3.2]$\,\AA{}; retrain with feature-space distances
(H1-feat).
U0 uses the same protocol as a negative control (Extended Data).

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Locked scalars: \texttt{outputs/nmi\_paper\_b/NUMBERS.json}.
Figures: \texttt{tools/plot\_nmi\_paper\_b\_figures.py}.
Null entry points: \texttt{tools/eval\_stead\_temporal\_null.py},
\texttt{tools/eval\_radhar\_temporal\_null.py},
\texttt{tools/train\_qm9\_huygens.py}.

\section*{Limitations}
We do not claim state-of-the-art on every metric; RadHAR accuracy is saturated.
STEAD sample-efficiency learning curves are not the primary knife of Paper~B.
Clinical EEG cohorts and rheology are demoted.
Paper~A regime maps are deferred.
QM9 H1 is competitive with, not strictly better than, M1 on \gap{}.
