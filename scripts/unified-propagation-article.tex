\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Complex spatiotemporal systems are often forecast with black-box predictors
whose internal propagation rules cannot be read as physics. We treat local
evolution as one computational object,
\(\hat{X}=P(X;G,\Theta,\alpha)\), in which delay, spatial coupling shape,
diffusive mixing and travel-time damping enter as continuous mechanism
coordinates \(\Theta\) with explicit physical roles, while \(\alpha\) measures
local departure from a domain coordinate \(\Theta^{\star}\). Forecast error is
used only as a probe. On the \stead{} seismic benchmark the preferred
coordinate is Huygens/wave: a physics-constrained probe with
\(192\,\mathrm{k}\) parameters matches \eqt{} on detection F1, improves S-wave
F1, and achieves the lowest P-wave timing error (P MAE \(0.0169\,\mathrm{s}\)
versus \(0.0462\,\mathrm{s}\) / \(0.0723\,\mathrm{s}\) for \eqt{} / \pnet{}).
Under the same prior we organise local-earthquake coda into five operational
facies and map a path residual \(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\) that retains structure
beyond scalar coda \(Q_c\). Scalp EEG and NOAA SST land at different
coordinates (instantaneous; graph diffusion). A telegrapher-inspired path
\(\Theta(\lambda)\) connects wave to diffusion through a damped-wave
intermediate by continuous change of the operator, not by proving Lyapunov
stability of trajectories. Local deviations \(\alpha\) are geometry-bound on
EEG and admit frozen reinjection closure on SST. The deepest empirical reading
of the primitive is therefore seismic Huygens facies, with cross-domain
coordinates showing that the same family can be re-landed elsewhere.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

Scientific machine learning often splits into two unsatisfactory habits. One
habit builds high-scoring predictors whose internal rules do not encode known
causal propagation, so forecast skill is hard to turn into discovery. The other
habit adds physics as a soft penalty or as a post-hoc explanation attached to an
opaque backbone, so the ``physics'' is correlative rather than constitutive of
the computation. Neither habit answers a basic comparative question: when two
systems both evolve on a sensor graph, do they prefer the same local propagator,
or different regimes of one family?

The question matters in seismology for a concrete reason. Velocity and
attenuation maps are mature, yet coda---how a path continues to ring after the
direct arrivals---is still routinely compressed into a scalar quality factor
\(Q_c\). That compression mixes absorption, scattering, multi-pathing and
near-receiver resonance. If local propagation is to serve as an instrument
rather than a scoreboard, one needs both a readable propagator and a discrete
language for path phenomenology that is not forced to be a single \(Q\). Scalp
EEG and climate sensor networks pose the dual problem: without a matched
protocol, one cannot tell whether they share delayed-wave coupling or select
other coordinates.

To make the comparison concrete, imagine three sensor networks that all look,
at first glance, like graphs with pairwise distances. A seismic array records
delayed arrivals whose envelopes continue to ring after \(S\). A scalp EEG cap
records nearly simultaneous envelope structure across centimetres of cortex at
the sampling rates used here. A sparse ocean-temperature sensor graph records
slow, spatially smoothed anomalies. If each domain is forced into its own neural
architecture, one cannot ask whether the difference is physical or merely
notational. If instead each domain is forced to choose a coordinate
\(\Theta^{\star}\) inside one operator \(P\), the difference becomes a
scientific claim that can be wrong.

Our answer is to write recursive local evolution
\begin{equation}
\text{local state}\rightarrow
\text{local response}\rightarrow
\text{neighbourhood messages }(A_{ij},\tau_{ij})\rightarrow
\text{secondary sources}
\end{equation}
as a single operator \(\hat{X}=P(X;G,\Theta,\alpha)\) (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}).
Here \(G\) is geometry (distances, sampling), \(\Theta\) is a vector of
mechanism coordinates with physical meaning, and \(\alpha\) is a multiplicative
local correction once a domain has chosen a coordinate \(\Theta^{\star}\).
Wave/Huygens, instantaneous coupling and graph diffusion are named corners of
\(\Theta\), not three unrelated model classes. Forecast error is only the probe
that makes those corners comparable; the scientific objects are
\(\Theta^{\star}\) and any validated \(\alpha\).

\paragraph{Physical meaning of \(\Theta\).}
The coordinates are not anonymous latents. Delay \(\delta\in[0,1]\) scales
geometric travel time into the lag used by the discrete propagator
(\(\delta=1\): finite-speed wave-like lag; \(\delta=0\): simultaneous update).
Spatial mix \(\sigma\) interpolates the radial amplitude law between a
wave/Huygens-like decay \(\propto e^{-\gamma r^{2}}/(r+\varepsilon)\) and a
graph-exponential \(e^{-r/\ell}\). Heat mix \(\eta\) blends toward a continuum
heat kernel, recovering diffusion-like spreading. Norm mix \(\nu\) softens
column versus row normalisation (mass conservation versus receiver-side
gain). Travel-time damping \(\zeta\) multiplies
\(e^{-\zeta\tau_{\mathrm{geo}}}\), a discrete stand-in for attenuation along
travel time---the ingredient that opens a damped-wave intermediate between pure
wave and pure diffusion. Auxiliary scales \((\gamma,\ell,c,\ldots)\) set length,
diffusivity and support; in the seismic probe the analogous Fresnel parameters
\((\gamma,\omega,c)\) remain Softplus-constrained and readable, with \(c\)
treated as a causal-support scale rather than an inverted medium velocity.
Together these axes make \(\Theta\) an interpretable chart of local coupling
physics. Interpretability does not imply that every coordinate is uniquely fixed
by forecast F1: nearby combinations can yield nearly the same score, so
observability must be checked by level sets and by tests that go beyond the
leaderboard (Methods).

\paragraph{Continuity versus Lyapunov stability.}
A one-parameter telegrapher-inspired path \(\Theta(\lambda)\) connects wave
(\(\lambda=0\)) to diffusion (\(\lambda=1\)) by continuously changing delay,
heat mix and damping, with peak damping at mid-path. What we demonstrate is
\emph{continuity of the operator family in mechanism space}: impulse-response
fingerprints and mean lag change smoothly along \(\lambda\), and named corners
recover legacy kernels. What we do \emph{not} claim is a Lyapunov stability
certificate for the discrete trajectories \(X_{t+1}=P(X_t)\). Continuity of
Green's-function-like parameters is not the same as asymptotic stability of an
equilibrium under a Lyapunov function. Continuum telegrapher equations admit
energy estimates in classical PDE theory; our discrete multi-sensor operator is
not given such a proof here. The continuum evidence should therefore be read as
a connected mechanism chart, not as a stability theorem.

In seismology the Huygens corner is first used as a multi-scale Fresnel probe
for phase picking, then---with ceilings frozen before geography is
inspected---as a readout of coda facies and path residuals. That reuse is the
empirical thickness of the story: the same computational prior that remains
competitive on \stead{} also organises how paths ring.

We report three results in that order. First, on \stead{} the Huygens probe is
competitive with \eqt{} and \pnet{} while supporting five operational coda facies
and path residuals beyond scalar \(Q_c\) (Table~\ref{tab:stead};
Fig.~\ref{fig:seismic}). Second, matched-budget ablations place EEG and SST at
other coordinates, and \(\Theta(\lambda)\) supplies a continuous bridge with a
damped-wave phase (Fig.~\ref{fig:cross}; Table~\ref{tab:domains}). Third,
\(\alpha\) is tested as geometry-bound departure from domain \(\Theta^{\star}\)
(Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha}). Intent, throughout, is mechanism identification under a
shared primitive---not a universal Huygens law for cortex and ocean, and not a
clinical biomarker claim.

\section*{Results}
% =============================================================================

\subsection*{A Huygens coordinate on STEAD: competitive timing under a physics-constrained probe}

If Huygens-type local propagation is to be more than a metaphor, it must remain
useful as a computational prior on a standard task.

\paragraph{What the probe computes.}
Operationally, three-component windows of length \(800\) covering
\(60\,\mathrm{s}\) are embedded into a width-\(64\) representation and passed
through fine and coarse Huygens--Fresnel blocks (causal windows of
\(8\,\mathrm{s}\) and \(20\,\mathrm{s}\)). Softplus-constrained kernel
parameters \((\gamma,\omega,c)\) control locality, oscillation and causal
support; a density field \(\rho(t)\) modulates secondary-source amplitudes.
Detection and P/S heads read out on the discrete time grid. The architectural
point for this paper is not the engineering checklist itself, but the fact that
the same frozen states that support competitive picks also export the coda
observables used below. In other words, Table~\ref{tab:stead} is the usefulness
gate that justifies reusing the probe as an instrument.

We therefore evaluate the
multi-scale Huygens--Fresnel probe (\pnf{}/\hnf{} family;
\(192\,\mathrm{k}\) parameters) on \stead{} under the usual
\(0.5\,\mathrm{s}\) tolerance protocol used for \eqt{}.
Table~\ref{tab:stead} reports full test-set detection and picking against
\eqt{} (\(\sim 377\,\mathrm{k}\) parameters) and \pnet{}
(\(\sim 269\,\mathrm{k}\)).

\pnf{} matches \eqt{} on detection F1 (\(0.9989\)), improves S-wave F1
(\(0.9770\) versus \(0.9731\) / \(0.9618\)), and achieves the lowest P-wave MAE
(\(0.0169\,\mathrm{s}\) on the discrete \(800\)-bin/\(60\,\mathrm{s}\) grid),
roughly \(2.7\times\) tighter than \eqt{} (\(0.0462\,\mathrm{s}\)) and
\(4.3\times\) tighter than \pnet{} (\(0.0723\,\mathrm{s}\)). It does not win
every cell: P-wave F1 (\(0.9852\)) trails \eqt{} (\(0.9897\)), and S-wave MAE
(\(0.0878\,\mathrm{s}\)) trails \pnet{} (\(0.0801\,\mathrm{s}\)). Looking at
precision and recall clarifies the trade. Recall remains strong across heads
(P-wave R \(0.9763\), S-wave R \(0.9643\)): when a genuine arrival is present,
the physics-constrained model tends to find it. The gap to \eqt{} is driven
mainly by precision (PNF P-wave P \(0.9942\) versus EQT \(0.9993\); S-wave P
\(0.9900\) versus \(0.9994\)). An unconstrained transformer is better at
suppressing false positives; a hard-wired Fresnel kernel restricts the
hypothesis class and, in return, keeps internal states readable.

The practical reading is therefore asymmetric and intentional. We do not need
every cell of Table~\ref{tab:stead} to be best in order to proceed. We need
enough usefulness, especially on timing, that reusing the same frozen states for
facies and path residuals is not an excuse for a weak picker. P-wave MAE is the
clearest usefulness signal; detection parity with \eqt{} and improved S-wave F1
reinforce that the prior is not merely decorative. The precision gap is the price
visible in the table, and we leave it visible.

We therefore read Table~\ref{tab:stead} as usefulness evidence under an
interpretable prior---especially on P timing---rather than as a claim that the
same checkpoint dominates every transfer setting. The scientific use of that
prior begins in the next subsection: the same frozen probe exports the
observables from which coda facies and path residuals are built.

\subsection*{Five coda facies under a Huygens prior: methodology, geography and path residuals}

\paragraph{Why facies belong in this story.}
The unified primitive says that seismology lands at a wave/Huygens coordinate.
If that landing is real, the probe should do more than pick arrivals: it should
expose readable path phenomenology. We therefore ask whether coda shape, under
the same prior, admits an interpretable discrete language that is richer than
scalar \(Q_c\) yet still tied to physically motivated observables. The five
facies below are that language. They are not a second, competing theory of
Earth; they are a readout of how paths ring once the computational prior is
fixed at Huygens.

\paragraph{What is physics-motivated, and what is operational.}
From a frozen multi-scale checkpoint we export three causal-chain observables
(Fig.~\ref{fig:seismic}a). \emph{Coda slope} summarises how quickly the envelope
decays after \(S\) and is the classical attenuation/scattering duration axis.
\emph{Onset sharpness} summarises how impulsive the first arrival is.
\emph{Delay-time density} \(\rho(\tau)\), after placing \(P\) at \(\tau=0\) and
\(S\) at \(\tau=1\), summarises whether energy arrives in one lobe or several;
multi-lobe structure is the multipath axis. These axes are physics-indexed: they
name attenuation duration, first-arrival impulsiveness and secondary-arrival
structure under a Huygens density. They are not anonymous embedding dimensions.

The five class names---impulsive fast decay, emergent, multipath, slow coda and
standard---are an \emph{operational partition} of that three-dimensional
observable space. The numerical ceilings
(Supplementary Table~\ref{stab:ceilings}: peak-count \(1.0\); coda-fast/slow
\(-0.198\) / \(-0.113\); onset-high/low \(0.877\) / \(0.733\)) were locked on
the validation set before any regional map was inspected. They are
\emph{not} \(k\)-means labels tuned on geography, and they are
\emph{not} outputs of a closed-form attenuation law. They are, however, close
to an objective approximation of the validation distributions: the coda-slope
ceilings sit at the approximate one-third / two-third quantiles of validation
coda slope (\(\approx-0.198\) / \(-0.113\)), and the onset ceilings sit at the
corresponding onset-sharpness quantiles (\(\approx0.733\) / \(0.877\)). A shallow
decision tree and a random forest fit on the same three validation observables
recover split thresholds in the same neighbourhood (dominant onset splits near
\(0.73\)--\(0.88\); dominant coda splits near \(-0.113\) and
\(-0.20\)), so the apparently precise decimals are better read as
\emph{quantile-aligned operational gates} than as hand-tuned oddities.
Because coda slope itself enters the ceilings, within-class coda contrasts
characterise the taxonomy; they are not an independent physical proof of the
cut-points.

\paragraph{How a waveform is labelled.}
Concretely, each local-earthquake trace is mapped into the causal frame, the
three observables are computed, and the frozen ceilings are applied in a fixed
order that yields one of the five names. Multipath is triggered when
\(\rho(\tau)\) is multi-peaked above the peak-count ceiling. Among the remaining
traces, onset ceilings separate impulsive from emergent first arrivals, while
coda-slope ceilings separate fast decay from slow ringing; the residual class is
called standard. The procedure is deterministic once ceilings are locked. It can
be audited, reproduced and criticised---unlike a clustering solution whose
cluster count and metric are tuned after seeing the map. We emphasise again that
the \emph{existence} of attenuation, impulsiveness and multipath axes is the
physical content; the \emph{particular numerical gates} are quantile-aligned
operational contracts with the reader, not post-hoc geographic tuning.

\paragraph{Reading the five facies.}
Impulsive fast decay combines a sharp onset with a steep post-\(S\) envelope:
energy arrives cleanly and leaves quickly. Emergent arrivals rise more
gradually, as if the first energy is distributed rather than concentrated.
Multipath waveforms show more than one lobe in \(\rho(\tau)\), consistent with
secondary arrivals or scattered packets that the delay density can resolve.
Slow coda keeps energy in the envelope long after \(S\), the ringing end-member.
Standard occupies the intermediate region of the same three axes. None of these
names is a tectonic province label; provinces enter only later, when geography
is examined as an independent test.

\paragraph{Geography as an out-of-ceiling test.}
The data basis is the \stead{} local-earthquake test catalogue
(\(>100{,}000\) traces after noise exclusion). Mapping facies geography---a
quantity that never entered the ceilings---shows continental organisation
(Cram\'er's \(V\approx0.37\), FDR-adjusted \(p<0.001\); Extended Data
Fig.~\ref{edfig:faciesgeo}). Impulsive fast decay enriches in the western United
States and northeast Pacific; slow coda concentrates in Central America and the
western Pacific. Class-mean envelopes and \(\rho(\tau)\) separate cleanly:
impulsive fast decay combines sharp onset with short coda; emergent rises
gradually; multipath shows multiple \(\rho(\tau)\) lobes; slow coda sustains
energy past \(S\); standard sits between these extremes. The geographic
organisation is evidence that the operational taxonomy is not arbitrary noise;
it is not evidence that Earth contains exactly five natural kinds.

\paragraph{Path-midpoint leftovers.}
To move from discrete facies to a continuous path field, coda slope is
distance-detrended to \(\beta\) against \(\log_{10}(\mathrm{dist}+1)\). A ridge
expectation (\(\lambda=8\)) then removes \(\log_{10}\) distance, depth,
magnitude, log-distance to the nearest Quaternary fault, and station fixed
effects. The leftover \(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\) is mapped at path midpoints. In
southern California (fixed stations; \(20\)--\(120\,\mathrm{km}\); structure
model \(R^{2}\approx0.27\)), two cells illustrate opposite behaviour under
same-station controls (Fig.~\ref{fig:seismic}b--d). Salton--Imperial midpoints
decay faster than the same stations' other paths (site-median
\(\Delta\beta\approx-0.032\); \(20\) stations; Cohen's \(d\approx-0.54\);
Mann--Whitney \(p=6.6\times10^{-32}\)). Eastern Transverse Ranges midpoints ring
longer (\(\Delta\beta\approx+0.025\); \(15\) stations; \(d\approx+0.68\);
\(p=2.5\times10^{-12}\)). Envelope stacks confirm the signs.

Independence checks ask whether the leftover is already explained by standard
summaries (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{edfig:independence}). Classical single-trace
\(q_c\) correlates with \(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\) (\(r=0.64\)); residualising on
\(q_c\) halves but does not erase the cell anomalies (Salton
\(\Delta\beta\): \(-0.030\rightarrow-0.014\); Transverse Ranges:
\(+0.040\rightarrow+0.019\)), and multipath versus slow-coda still separate
inside matched \(q_c\) terciles. Berg et al.\ upper-crust \(V_S\) is nearly
uncorrelated (\(r=-0.08\)); the two cells occupy the same \(V_S\) band with
opposite \(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\)\cite{berg2021}. Lin \& Jordan direct-wave
\(Q_S\) is likewise near-orthogonal (\(r=-0.07\)); removing \(Q_S\) leaves
\(\Delta\beta\) essentially intact, and Salton can combine relatively high
direct-wave \(Q_S\) with faster coda decay\cite{lin2023}. Near-null Pearson
correlations can also arise from resolution mismatch or from coda versus
direct-wave sensitivity. The intended reading is modest: the leftover retains
information not captured by the examined linear summaries---not that coda is
physically independent of all structure.

\paragraph{Why same-station controls matter.}
A path-midpoint map can always be confounded by station site effects: some
stations simply sit in quieter or noisier local geology. The same-station
contrast asks a sharper question. Fix the station, and ask whether paths whose
midpoints fall inside a candidate cell differ systematically from that
station's other paths. When the answer is yes with a coherent sign across many
stations, the leftover is harder to dismiss as a site term. Salton--Imperial and
eastern Transverse Ranges are reported because they pass that bar with opposite
signs, not because they were the only cells inspected during exploration.

\paragraph{Physical intuition without over-interpretation.}
A negative \(\Delta\beta\) means faster envelope decay than the same station
records elsewhere: energy is absorbed or de-trapped more quickly along those
paths. A positive \(\Delta\beta\) means longer ringing. Fluids, damage zones,
basin reverberation and volcanic scatterers are all plausible carriers; \stead{}
alone does not uniquely identify them. The claim at this stage is the existence
of a station-controlled leftover organised in space and not absorbed by the
examined \(q_c\), \(V_S\) and \(Q_S\) summaries.

\paragraph{What the classical coda control clarifies.}
Because \(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\) is computed from a raw-envelope slope in an
\(S\)-timed window, one can recompute the identical slope using \stead{} catalog
\(S\) picks (\(n=4000\) SoCal traces). Catalog-timed and probe-timed slopes
agree closely (\(r=0.96\)); \(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\) maps agree at \(r=0.93\);
same-station cell signs are essentially unchanged. Predicting probe
\(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\) from classical coda features already yields five-fold
OOS \(R^{2}=0.711\); adding probe-specific latents (peak count, onset sharpness,
facies indicators) yields \(\Delta R^{2}\approx0\). This control does not
contradict the unified story. It clarifies the contribution: the spatial
leftover is classical coda geography that the Huygens probe reproduces and
times without a separate catalog, while the distinctive scientific product is
the \(\rho(\tau)\)-indexed five-mode taxonomy under that prior, together with
the competitive structured picker in Table~\ref{tab:stead}. We keep the facies
because they extend the Huygens coordinate into a discrete path language; we do
not sell the map as a uniquely neural residual field.

\paragraph{Out-of-region consistency.}
The identical frozen ceilings and residualisation on Cascadia recover a
Salton-signed faster-decay residual near Mt St Helens--Cowlitz (\(8\) stations;
site-median \(\Delta\beta=-0.014\); \(88\%\) sign agreement; \(d=-0.30\);
\(p=5.6\times10^{-14}\); jackknife stable under the Methods rule) and an
opposite ringing residual near Seattle (\(\Delta\beta=+0.045\); \(d=+0.83\);
Extended Data Fig.~\ref{edfig:cascadia}). We present these as consistency
examples selected as geographic end-members, and we state a concrete follow-up
prediction: new paths with midpoints in the Salton cell should show same-station
\(\Delta\beta\le-0.02\) once \(\ge8\) stations are available; Seattle predicts
the opposite inequality. A complete spatial multiple-testing catalog is not
claimed here.

\subsection*{Cross-domain mechanism coordinates and a continuous operator path}

\paragraph{Matched-budget regime identity.}
If \(P\) is truly one family, different domains should be allowed to land at
different corners without rewriting the architecture. Under matched training
budgets, shared local-response capacity and geometry controls, scalp EEG prefers
instantaneous coupling (lag \(\equiv 0\)) over delayed Huygens, whereas NOAA SST
sensor networks prefer graph diffusion (Fig.~\ref{fig:cross}a;
Table~\ref{tab:domains}). On merged EEG ablations the instantaneous prior
reaches mean MSE \(\approx0.064\) with geometry-relative degradation
\(\approx+15\%\); graph diffusion is second and delayed wave is worse. On SST
the preferred prior is graph diffusion (geometry-relative degradation
\(\approx0.76\); diffusion family preferred). On \stead{}, earlier work already
favoured delayed wave/Huygens over free adjacency, with only weak
geometry-sensitive \(\alpha\) and without supporting a travel-time residual map
as the scientific product.

\paragraph{How to read the domain triangle.}
A useful way to hold the three landings together is as a triangle in mechanism
space. \stead{} occupies the delayed-wave corner: finite lag matters, and the
Huygens amplitude law is the prior that remains task-useful. Scalp EEG occupies
the instantaneous corner: at the scales probed here, forcing finite travel-time
lag hurts more than it helps, so the preferred coupling is geometric but not
delayed. SST occupies the graph-diffusion corner: spatial smoothing on the
sensor graph outperforms both delayed wave and lag-free wave-amplitude shapes.
The triangle is the empirical content of unification. It does not say that every
future dataset must land on one of these three vertices; it says that when a
dataset does prefer one of them, that preference is a coordinate of the same
family.

\paragraph{Why matched budgets matter.}
Without matched budgets, a larger free model can always look like a ``better
regime'' simply by fitting noise. We therefore compare priors under shared
response capacity and shared optimisation effort, and we ask whether geometry
still matters when distances are scrambled. A prior that wins on error but
collapses under geometry controls is treated as a fit residue, not as a
mechanism coordinate. That discipline is what allows Table~\ref{tab:domains} to
be read as physics rather than as a leaderboard of architectures.

\paragraph{Named corners as special cases.}
Evaluating the unified operator at the named corners recovers the legacy
kernels used in those ablations, and \(\alpha\equiv1\) probes on real EEG and
SST windows recover the same preferred labels (Fig.~\ref{fig:cross}b). Piecewise
paths among corners change amplitude and lag without pathological jumps under
the continuity gates in Methods. \stead{}, EEG and SST therefore appear as
distinct coordinates of one family. On synthetic systems with known
ground-truth propagators, an \(\alpha\equiv1\) decision rule recovers regimes
with perfect confusion-matrix accuracy, showing identifiability in principle;
broader real-domain fingerprint gates remain open.

\paragraph{Telegrapher path \(\Theta(\lambda)\).}
To connect wave and diffusion by a single physically motivated dial rather than
by a free blend of unrelated models, we use a telegrapher-inspired path
\(\Theta(\lambda)\) with
\[
\delta=(1-\lambda)^{2},\qquad
\eta=\lambda^{2},\qquad
\zeta=4\lambda(1-\lambda).
\]
At \(\lambda=0\) the operator is wave-like (full delay, no heat mix, no
damping). At \(\lambda=1\) it is diffusive (vanishing delay, heat mix on). At
mid-path, travel-time damping peaks while residual delay remains---the discrete
damped-wave phase suggested by Cattaneo/telegrapher relaxation. Empirically,
mean lag on the primary wave-to-diffusion path collapses monotonically
(\(0.523\rightarrow0\)), and phase labels traverse wave-like \(\rightarrow\)
damped-wave \(\rightarrow\) diffusive (Fig.~\ref{fig:cross}c,d). This is
evidence that the mechanism chart is path-connected. As stated in the
Introduction, it is not a Lyapunov proof for discrete trajectories. Linear
mixtures of several free primitives are left aside: without additional
identifiability structure they would blur the very coordinates we are trying to
read.

\paragraph{What the impulse-response fingerprint shows.}
As \(\lambda\) increases, the discrete impulse response loses delayed side-lobes
and becomes more local in time, consistent with lag collapse. Mid-path damping
suppresses long travel-time contributions even while some delay remains, which
is why we label that regime damped-wave rather than forcing a binary
wave-versus-diffusion vocabulary. The fingerprint is therefore a qualitative
chart of the operator family: readers can see the intermediate without needing
a stability theorem.

\subsection*{Local deviation \(\alpha\) from domain \(\Theta^{\star}\)}

Once a domain has chosen \(\Theta^{\star}\), residual error may still be
structured. We treat \(\alpha\) as a multiplicative correction to
\(K(\Theta^{\star})\), not as a universal leftover defined against Huygens in
every domain. The question is whether that correction is bound to geometry and,
when compressed into a named hypothesis, whether it survives frozen reinjection.

\paragraph{EEG: geometry-bound \(\alpha\).}
Under EEG's instantaneous coordinate, a low-dimensional distance-structured
\(\alpha\) improves over \(\alpha\equiv1\), and the improvement collapses under
counterfactuals that break geometry: placing \(\alpha\) on scrambled electrode
distances raises relative error by about \(+82\%\); permuting \(\alpha\) across
edges raises it by about \(+64\%\); the distance-slope sign is stable across
seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha}a). An independent healthy-lifespan LEMON cohort
reproduces the pattern (instantaneous MSE \(0.076\) versus wave \(0.098\);
wrong-geometry relative degradation about \(+67\%\), CI \([0.37,1.09]\),
\(90\) subjects; Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha}b). Forecast MSE by itself is not the
argument: unconstrained baselines can still win raw error under some protocols.
The mechanistic claim rests on the geometry counterfactuals.

\paragraph{What ``geometry-bound'' means in practice.}
Suppose one fits a smooth function of electrode distance that multiplies the
instantaneous coupling amplitudes. If that function is merely absorbing
unmodelled variance, it should still help after the distances are replaced by
scrambled distances or after the multipliers are randomly reassigned across
edges. If instead the function encodes geometry, those interventions should
hurt. The EEG counterfactuals implement exactly that test. The large relative
degradations under wrong geometry and edge permutation are therefore the
mechanistic evidence; the improvement over \(\alpha\equiv1\) is only the
motivation for fitting \(\alpha\) in the first place.

\paragraph{What reinjection adds beyond fitting.}
Fitting \(\alpha\) shows that residual structure exists. Reinjection asks a
harder question: can a named, frozen functional form---here a range-dependent
gain---carry that structure to held-out seeds without refitting the mechanism
coordinates? On SST the answer is yes for the tested form. On EEG the same
family only partially tracks free \(\alpha\). The contrast is informative:
closure depth is part of the scientific reading of \(\alpha\), not a nuisance
to be hidden. It suggests that local deviation fields are real, but that the
right low-dimensional hypothesis for those fields is domain-dependent.

\paragraph{SST versus EEG: reinjection depth differs.}
On SST under graph diffusion, a range-dependent gain mined from \(\alpha\) can
be frozen and reinjected on held-out seeds: the named form beats wrong and
sign-flipped controls without refitting mechanism parameters
(Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha}c). A companion consistency map aligns near-boost and
mid-cut structure with SST contrast, with the important caveat that the scale is
the sensor graph rather than a geophysical diffusivity in Rd. Transferring the
same range-dependent family to EEG only partially tracks free \(\alpha\)
(about one-fifth of windows near-full; about half moderate; wrong forms still
often beat named forms; Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha}d). Closure depth is therefore
regime-dependent (Table~\ref{tab:domains}). Exploratory associations between EEG
leftovers and clinical cognition scores are summarised in Supplementary
Note~\ref{snote:clinical} for completeness of the EEG thread; they are
underpowered on the primary Spearman and are not used to carry the paper's
argument.

\section*{Discussion}
% =============================================================================

The through-line is a single local-propagation primitive read at different
depths. On \stead{}, Huygens is both task-useful (Table~\ref{tab:stead}) and
scientifically productive: it yields physics-indexed coda observables, an
operational five-mode facies language, and path residuals that retain structure
beyond the linear summaries we tested (Fig.~\ref{fig:seismic}). Cross-domain
comparisons then show that the same primitive can land at instantaneous and
graph-diffusion coordinates, with \(\Theta(\lambda)\) supplying a continuous
operator path and a damped-wave intermediate (Fig.~\ref{fig:cross}). Local
\(\alpha\) measures departure from those landings, with geometry binding on EEG
and deeper reinjection closure on SST (Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha}).

This is why the facies belong with the unified function rather than against it.
The axes (coda duration, onset, multipath delay structure) inherit their meaning
from wave/Huygens computation; the thresholds are operational gates that make
the taxonomy reproducible. The classical coda control further keeps the claim
honest: much of the continuous map is classical envelope geography, while the
Huygens contribution is the readable discrete language and the structured
picker that times it. Huygens is therefore the right interpretable principle
\emph{for this seismic reading}---not a claim that cortex or ocean must obey
the same corner.

A broader implication follows for scientific machine learning. When a paper
proposes a ``physics-informed'' module, two questions should be asked in order.
First: which physically meaningful coordinates does the module expose, and can
those coordinates be compared across domains under a matched protocol? Second:
when the module is specialised to one domain---as Huygens is specialised here to
seismic facies---does the specialisation inherit its meaning from those
coordinates, or does it quietly become a new black box? The present work tries
to keep both questions visible. The unified operator answers the first; the
facies taxonomy answers the second for seismology, with thresholds stated as quantile-aligned operational gates.

Two limits should be stated where they matter. First, operator-path continuity
along \(\Theta(\lambda)\) is not a Lyapunov stability theorem for discrete
trajectories; proving energy decay or asymptotic stability remains open.
Second, Cascadia is offered as consistency rather than a finished spatial
replication catalog, and SST consistency maps live on the sensor graph.
Extending \(\Theta\) with reaction or advection terms is natural within the same
interface; blending several free primitives without new identifiability
structure is not.

Finally, the classical coda control and the operational facies thresholds should
be read together. The control prevents a romantic story in which neural latents
invent a new map of Earth from nothing. The thresholds prevent a different
romantic story in which clustering discovers five natural kinds after the map is
seen. What remains is more ordinary and, we think, more useful: a Huygens
computational prior, a reproducible discrete language for coda shape, and a path
residual that can be compared to published structure products.

\section*{Methods}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Unified operator and physical coordinates.}
Let \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times T}\) be the field on \(N\) sensors. Geometry \(G\)
gives pairwise distances \(r_{ij}\) and sampling \(\mathrm{d}t\). Mechanism
coordinates
\[
\Theta=(\delta,\sigma,\eta,\nu,\zeta;\gamma,\ell,c,\ldots)
\]
have the physical roles stated in the Introduction: \(\delta\) scales geometric
travel time into lag; \(\sigma\) mixes wave-like and graph-exponential radial
laws; \(\eta\) mixes toward a heat kernel; \(\nu\) softens column/row
normalisation; \(\zeta\) applies travel-time damping
\(e^{-\zeta\tau_{\mathrm{geo}}}\). Geometric travel time is
\(\tau_{\mathrm{geo},ij}=\mathrm{round}(r_{ij}/(c\,\mathrm{d}t))\); lags are
\(\tau_{ij}=\mathrm{round}(\delta\cdot\tau_{\mathrm{geo},ij})\). Neighbour drive
is aggregated with amplitudes \(A(G;\Theta)\) and lags \(\tau\), then combined
with the self state by a shared local response \(R\). Reality uses
\(A\odot\alpha\). Named corners recover the legacy wave, instantaneous and graph
diffusion kernels used in domain ablations.

\paragraph{Aggregation and update.}
With amplitudes \(A_{ij}\) and lags \(\tau_{ij}\), the neighbour aggregate at
sensor \(j\) and time \(t\) is
\[
\mathrm{agg}_j(t)=\sum_i (\alpha\odot A)_{ij}\, X_i(t-\tau_{ij}),
\]
and the local response updates \(X_j(t)\leftarrow R\big(X_j(t),\mathrm{agg}_j(t)\big)\).
The same \(R\) class is shared across regime comparisons so that differences in
forecast error can be attributed to \((A,\tau)\) rather than to an arbitrarily
more expressive decoder. Named corners set \((\delta,\sigma,\eta,\nu,\zeta)\) to
the wave, instantaneous or graph-diffusion limits described above; auxiliary
scales are matched so that those corners reproduce the legacy kernels used in
domain studies.

\paragraph{Seismic architecture in five stages.}
(1)~Source embedding maps \(x\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times800\times3}\) to width \(64\)
real/imaginary pathways. (2)~Multi-scale Huygens blocks run fine
(\(8\,\mathrm{s}\) causal window) and coarse (\(20\,\mathrm{s}\)) branches with
three WaveBlocks each and branch-specific \((\gamma,\omega,c)\).
(3)~A causal network estimates density \(\rho(t)\), Softplus-constrained, which
modulates secondary-source amplitudes. (4)~Task branches apply a final
propagation block with P- and S-sensitive pathways. (5)~Heads emit detection
logits and pick probabilities on the \(800\)-bin grid. Training uses Adam with
cosine annealing, batch size \(32\), about \(50\) epochs (best validation near
epoch \(37\)). The loss combines detection and P/S cross-entropy with a
P-before-S order term and mild regularisers on consistency, wrong-peak mass and
\(\rho\) sparsity. Full-test comparison uses the \stead{} test split and
\(0.5\,\mathrm{s}\) tolerance; baseline pickers are evaluated under the same
tolerance protocol.

\paragraph{Computing \(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\).}
For each trace, let \(s\) be the coda-decay slope on the envelope. Distance
detrending yields \(\beta\) as the residual of \(s\) on
\(\log_{10}(\mathrm{dist}+1)\). A ridge regression with penalty \(8\) then
predicts \(\beta\) from \(\log_{10}\) distance, depth, magnitude,
\(\log(1+d_{\mathrm{fault}})\) to the nearest Quaternary fault, and station
fixed effects. The residual of that regression is \(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\),
assigned to the geographic path midpoint and aggregated on \(0.25^\circ\) cells
for maps. Same-station tests compare, at each station, traces whose midpoints
fall inside a candidate cell to traces whose midpoints fall outside the cell and
a half-cell buffer.

\paragraph{Cascadia filters.}
Cascadia analyses use \stead{} paths inside
\(45.2\)--\(48.8^\circ\mathrm{N}\), \(124.6\)--\(120.2^\circ\mathrm{W}\), with
minimum station-year filters (\(2\) station-years / \(8\) traces). After
distance and fixed-station filters, \(16\mathrm{k}\) labelled traces reduce to
\(9393\). Jackknives drop top source bins, cap traces per bin, leave years out
(\(2005\)--\(2018\)), and apply magnitude floors; stability requires
\(\ge80\%\) of qualifying tests to keep the expected \(\Delta\beta\) sign with
\(p<0.05\) and at least eight stations.

\paragraph{EEG and SST protocols (summary).}
EEG regime comparisons and \(\alpha\) tests use eyes-closed multi-channel
windows with subject-level bootstrap for counterfactual intervals; LEMON is an
independent healthy-lifespan cohort analysed with within-cohort residualisation
discipline where clinical leftovers are discussed. SST regime comparisons and
range-dependent reinjection use sensor-network windows under graph-diffusion
priors; reinjection freezes the gain form on one seed and evaluates held-out
seeds against wrong and sign-flipped forms without refitting mechanism
parameters. Detailed cohort splits and window lengths are given in the
Supplementary Information.

\paragraph{Continuity gates and the Lyapunov distinction.}
Along \(\Theta(\lambda)\) we require finite steps in amplitude and probe MSE,
monotone collapse of mean lag on the wave-to-diffusion path, and a mid-path
damping peak for the damped-wave label (observed lag
\(0.523\rightarrow0\)). These gates certify continuous change of the discrete
operator's impulse response. They do not construct a Lyapunov function \(V(X)\)
with \(\Delta V\le0\) along \(X_{t+1}=P(X_t)\), nor do they import continuum
energy estimates for the telegrapher PDE into the multi-sensor discrete system.
We therefore report operator-path continuity, not Lyapunov stability.

\paragraph{Matched-budget regime ablation.}
EEG and SST compare wave, instantaneous, diffusion and graph-diffusion priors
under identical local-response capacity, optimisation budget and freeze policy
on \(R\). Geometry controls scramble or refit against wrong distances while
holding capacity fixed. Preferred \(\Theta^{\star}\) is the lowest-error prior
that remains geometry-sensitive. Corner recovery requires the unified family,
evaluated at the named point, to match the legacy kernel behaviour used in those
ablations.

\paragraph{\stead{} Huygens probe and timing benchmark.}
The production seismic probe is a multi-scale Huygens--Fresnel network
(\(\approx192\,\mathrm{k}\) parameters). Inputs are three-component \stead{}
windows of length \(800\) covering \(60\,\mathrm{s}\) (native \(100\,\mathrm{Hz}\)).
A width-\(64\) embedding feeds fine/coarse WaveBlocks (causal windows
\(8\,\mathrm{s}\) / \(20\,\mathrm{s}\); three blocks per scale) with
Softplus-constrained Fresnel parameters \((\gamma,\omega,c)\); \(c\) is a
support scale. Detection and P/S heads decode on the discrete grid (bin width
\(\approx0.075\,\mathrm{s}\)). Training uses Adam with cosine annealing, batch
size \(32\), about \(50\) epochs (best validation near epoch \(37\)), and a
composite loss of detection/P/S cross-entropy with order and mild physical
regularisers. Full-test metrics use tolerance \(0.5\,\mathrm{s}\) and the
\eqt{} threshold protocol. Authoritative numbers are those in
Table~\ref{tab:stead}.

\paragraph{Facies observables and operational ceilings.}
Morphology uses one frozen multi-scale checkpoint. Observables:
(i)~envelope coda slope in an \(S\)-timed window;
(ii)~onset sharpness;
(iii)~peak count / lobe structure in \(\rho(\tau)\) after causal resampling with
\(P\) at \(\tau=0\) and \(S\) at \(\tau=1\). Class assignment is rule-based on
ceilings locked on validation before test-set geography
(Supplementary Table~\ref{stab:ceilings}). Axes are physics-indexed. Thresholds
are operational but quantile-aligned: on the validation export, coda-fast/slow
ceilings match the \(\approx33\%/67\%\) quantiles of coda slope, and
onset-low/high ceilings match the corresponding onset quantiles; shallow CART /
random-forest splits on the same three features recover thresholds in the same
neighbourhood (Supplementary Note~\ref{snote:ceilings}). Geography association
uses Cram\'er's \(V\) with FDR adjustment on the \stead{} local-earthquake test
set after noise exclusion.

\paragraph{Structure residual, independence and classical control.}
Distance-detrend coda slope to \(\beta\); ridge-residualise
(\(\lambda=8\)) on \(\log_{10}\) distance, depth, magnitude,
\(\log(1+\mathrm{fault\ distance})\) and station fixed effects; map
\(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\) at midpoints (SoCal \(20\)--\(120\,\mathrm{km}\)).
Same-station tests compare in-cell versus out-of-cell paths with a geographic
buffer; discovery requires \(\ge8\) stations and \(p<0.05\). Classical \(q_c\)
uses a mean-square envelope, 21-sample smoother, and the slope of
\(\log_{10}E(t)\) from the first post-peak drop below \(55\%\) of peak energy
through \(+8\,\mathrm{s}\). Berg \(V_S\) and Lin \& Jordan \(Q_S\) are sampled
as \(0\)--\(8\,\mathrm{km}\) column means at midpoints (resolved nodes only for
\(Q_S\)). Cascadia uses the same frozen ceilings; jackknives (source-bin /
magnitude / year) are called stable when \(\ge80\%\) of qualifying tests keep
the expected sign with \(p<0.05\) and \(n_{\mathrm{sites}}\ge8\). Catalog-timed
classical control recomputes the identical envelope slope with catalog \(S\) on
\(n=4000\) SoCal traces.

\paragraph{\(\alpha\) geometry and reinjection tests.}
\(\alpha\) is fit only under the preferred prior. EEG tests compare
\(\alpha\) against \(\alpha\equiv1\), channel shuffles, geometry-scramble
refits, evaluation of \(\alpha\) on wrong geometry, edge permutation, and
distance-slope sign stability across seeds (subject bootstrap). LEMON repeats
preferred-regime and key geometry tests on an independent healthy cohort. SST
range-dependent gain: mine on one seed, freeze the functional form, reinject on
held-out seeds against wrong/sign-flip controls, without refitting mechanism
parameters. EEG transfer of the same family is graded by the fraction of windows
near-full or moderate and by whether wrong forms still win.

\paragraph{Observability of mechanism coordinates.}
Forecast skill alone does not uniquely identify \(\Theta\). In particular, the
same dataset can admit a neighbourhood of parameter combinations with nearly
indistinguishable detection/picking F1 (or probe MSE). We therefore treat
task metrics as usefulness probes rather than as injective maps from score to
coordinates. Synthetic impulse-response recovery already illustrates the
asymmetry: Fresnel locality and oscillation \((\gamma,\omega)\) are recovered
tightly, whereas the causal-support scale \(c\) is only weakly identified
(mean relative error \(\approx0.30\) on one-dimensional impulses). For the
unified coordinates, a brief iso-performance (level-set) check on validation
windows asks which nearby \((\delta,\sigma,\eta,\zeta)\) keep probe error
within a small tolerance of the preferred corner; extended ridges in that
level set are reported as limited local observability, not as a failure of the
primitive (Supplementary Note~\ref{snote:observability}). Mechanism claims
accordingly rest on named-corner recovery, geometry counterfactuals and
readable latents---not on uniqueness of an F1 maximiser.

\paragraph{Compute resources.}
Seismic training and evaluation, EEG/SST regime ablations and \(\alpha\)
counterfactual runs were performed on a workstation with a single NVIDIA
GeForce RTX~3080 GPU (10\,GB), supported by host CPU preprocessing. Full-test
\stead{} scoring and facies exports are inference-time workloads on the same
class of device; large catalogue residualisation steps are CPU-bound.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Same-station \(\Delta\beta\): site-median, Cohen's \(d\), Mann--Whitney \(p\).
EEG counterfactuals: subject-bootstrap intervals on relative degradation.
Clinical Spearman in Supplementary Note~\ref{snote:clinical} is two-sided and
is not used as a primary claim.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note: Physics Decoder}
\label{snote:decoder}
A lightweight decoder on frozen seismic latents can initialise layered
\((v_P,v_S)\) models for travel-time misfit reduction on a reported protocol
(\(69.6\%\) of \(n=500\) events improve; mean misfit \(11.22\rightarrow3.08\)).
Provenance and geographic-prior controls are only partially documented; we treat
the decoder as an optional initialisation aid, not as a pillar of the unified
argument.

\subsection*{Facies ceilings as quantile-aligned gates}
\label{snote:ceilings}
The three observables (coda slope, onset sharpness, \(\rho(\tau)\) lobe count)
name attenuation duration, impulsiveness and multipath delay structure under a
Huygens density. On the validation export used to lock ceilings, the coda-fast
and coda-slow bounds coincide with the approximate \(33\%\) and \(67\%\)
quantiles of coda slope (\(-0.198\) and \(-0.113\)), while the onset-low and
onset-high bounds coincide with the corresponding onset-sharpness quantiles
(\(0.733\) and \(0.877\)). The peak-count ceiling separates single-lobed from
multi-lobed \(\rho(\tau)\). A depth-limited decision tree and a random forest
trained on these three validation features place impurity-minimising splits in
the same neighbourhood (onset near \(0.73\)--\(0.88\); coda near \(-0.113\) and
\(\approx-0.20\)). We therefore describe the decimals as
\emph{quantile-aligned operational gates}: more objective than arbitrary
hand-tuning, yet still an operational partition rather than a law of nature.
Ceilings remain frozen before geographic inspection; replacing them by
clustering after seeing maps would break the paper's logic.

\subsection*{Observability and iso-performance neighbourhoods}
\label{snote:observability}
Let \(\mathcal{L}(\Theta)\) denote a probe loss (pick F1 on \stead{}, or
envelope MSE on EEG/SST). An iso-performance set at tolerance \(\varepsilon\) is
\[
\{\Theta' : |\mathcal{L}(\Theta')-\mathcal{L}(\Theta^{\star})|\le\varepsilon\}.
\]
If that set is a thin island around \(\Theta^{\star}\), local observability is
strong; if it forms an extended ridge, several parameter combinations are
operationally equivalent under the probe. Empirically, Fresnel
\((\gamma,\omega)\) behave more like islands under synthetic impulse recovery,
whereas \(c\) behaves like a ridge (weak identification). For unified
\((\delta,\sigma,\eta,\zeta)\), nearby points along \(\Theta(\lambda)\) change
the operator continuously and therefore populate elongated level sets by
construction; we report that geometry rather than claiming a unique F1
maximiser. This is why geometry counterfactuals and named-corner recovery are
required to elevate a coordinate from ``good score'' to ``mechanism reading''.

\subsection*{Loss terms for the seismic probe (summary)}
The training objective combines detection and P/S cross-entropy with a soft
P-before-S order penalty, consistency regularisers on noise windows, a
wrong-peak penalty, mild \(\rho\) sparsity, and a weak prior on kernel
parameters. Relative weights are fixed for the canonical checkpoint. Picking
usefulness is optimised directly; facies analyses reuse that frozen checkpoint
without re-optimising ceilings against geography.

\subsection*{Ridge design matrix for \(\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\)}
Predictors entering the ridge expectation are
\(\log_{10}(\mathrm{distance}+1)\), event depth, magnitude,
\(\log(1+d_{\mathrm{fault}})\) to the nearest densified Quaternary fault
polyline (local kilometre scaling), and station fixed effects. The ridge penalty
is \(8\). SoCal maps use fixed stations and path distances
\(20\)--\(120\,\mathrm{km}\). Midpoints are geographic means of source and
receiver coordinates. Grid aggregation for display uses \(0.25^\circ\) cells;
statistical cell tests use the same-station protocol rather than grid-cell
\(t\)-tests alone.

\subsection*{Facies assignment rule (operational)}
Given observables \((n_{\rho}, s_{\mathrm{coda}}, a_{\mathrm{onset}})\) and the
frozen ceilings in Table~\ref{stab:ceilings}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(n_{\rho}\) exceeds the peak-count ceiling, label \textbf{multipath}.
\item Else if onset is below the low ceiling, prefer \textbf{emergent} when
consistent with the joint rule used in the frozen pipeline; if onset is above
the high ceiling and coda is below the fast bound, label
\textbf{impulsive fast decay}.
\item Else if coda slope exceeds the slow bound, label \textbf{slow coda}.
\item Else label \textbf{standard}.
\end{enumerate}
The exact joint Boolean used in the frozen export is held fixed with the
ceilings; the point of listing the rule is to make clear that labels are
deterministic operational decisions on physical axes, not cluster IDs.

\subsection*{Operator-path continuity versus Lyapunov stability}
Along \(\Theta(\lambda)\) we monitor mean lag, travel-time damping and
impulse-response shape. Continuity gates require finite steps and monotone lag
collapse on the wave-to-diffusion path. A Lyapunov argument would instead
require a function \(V(X)\) decreasing along discrete trajectories of \(P\).
No such \(V\) is constructed here. Continuum telegrapher energy estimates
motivate the path but do not transfer automatically to the multi-sensor
discrete operator.

\subsection*{Supplementary EEG/SST window notes}
EEG analyses use eyes-closed multi-channel segments; subject-level splits keep
counterfactual intervals from pooling dependent windows as if they were
independent subjects. LEMON is analysed as a healthy-lifespan cohort with
within-cohort residualisation when clinical leftovers are discussed. SST
analyses use sparse sensor graphs; range-dependent gains are therefore
statements about the sensor graph, not about a continuum diffusivity field in
geophysical coordinates.
